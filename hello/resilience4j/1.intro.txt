Step 1: What problem does Resilience4j solve? (Big picture)
================================================================
In microservices, your service often calls other services (REST, GraphQL, DB, Kafka, etc.).
When a downstream service:
- is slow
- is down
- starts timing out
- intermittently fails

👉 your service can:
- block threads
- cascade failures
- cause timeouts upstream
- trigger retries → even more load (thundering herd)
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|   Resilience4j adds protective wrappers around such calls.                    |
|   Think of it as defensive infrastructure code, but done declaratively.       |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------+

    Your Service -> [ Resilience4j ] -> Downstream API

Resilience4j decides:

❌ Should I even call the downstream?
⏱️ How long should I wait?
🔁 Should I retry?
🧯 Should I fail fast and use fallback?

What Resilience4j gives you (high level)
----------------------------------------
Circuit Breaker → stop calling a failing service
Retry → retry transient failures
TimeLimiter → fail if call takes too long
Bulkhead → limit concurrent calls
RateLimiter → limit request rate

Why teams like it (vs Netflix Hystrix)
------------------------------------
Lightweight (no thread pools forced)
Pure Java (functional style)
Spring-friendly annotations
Actuator + Micrometer support
